# Related work and scope

The task studied here is to prepare the quantum output of a specified vacuum dilation, while discarding the final system. The approximation must hold for every input, including inputs entangled with an untouched reference. The charged resource is the number of calls to the supplied, phase-referenced controlled unitary oracle. This output contract matters: preparing the reduced system, preparing its complementary output, and sampling a measured trajectory are different simulation tasks.

## Lindbladian simulation and phase estimation

[Gao, Ji, and Liu][gao2026] develop Hamiltonian twirling representations of Lindbladian semigroups using the Lévy–Khintchine structure. Their treatment includes Gaussian dephasing, compound-Poisson jumps, phase-estimation interpretations of purifications, and simulation lower bounds. Section III discusses continuous-variable phase estimation, Appendix C.3 treats Poisson twirling, and Appendix D.2 proves a linear-time reduced-system lower bound for queries to a block encoding of the Hamiltonian. The underlying unitary-jump dynamics overlap with the present model; the oracle definitions must still be distinguished.

[Shang et al.][shang2025] establish quadratic fast forwarding for the reduced-system semigroup generated by a Hermitian jump operator. Theorem 2 and Appendix E use concentration of a symmetric random walk in forward and backward Hamiltonian evolution; Appendix I extends the result to an appropriate commuting class of generators. These results already establish the connection between diffusive fluctuations, phase estimation, and square-root simulation cost.

[Borras and Marvian][borras2026] construct trajectory-based simulation algorithms with additive time and precision dependence in the number of jump-operator queries for a restricted Lindbladian class. Their target is the reduced-system channel. Keeping a coherent environment output instead of discarding the trajectory registers changes which relative amplitudes must be reproduced.

[Shang, An, and Shao][shang2026] give a reduced-system algorithm for unitary jumps with additive query dependence on time and precision; their structured fast-forwarding result reduces parallel depth. Appendix B.1, Eqs. (51)–(53), explicitly prepares a coherent truncated Poisson count and chronological jump-word purification before tracing its ancillas. Retaining that purification supplies the natural linear-query encoded-record construction. Identifying the word code with the specified continuous field, and tracing an individual bath correctly, require the field embedding used here. The generic coherent trajectory preparation itself is established prior work.

The Gaussian phase-clock purification also has a precise relation to this output task. A known rescaling into the momentum representation maps the wavefunction $`\sqrt{p_\tau(s)}e^{-ihs}`$ to the real coherent state $`|\sqrt{\tau}\,h\rangle`$. Applying this identity to the commuting Hermitian operators $`A=\mathrm{Re}U`$ and $`B=\mathrm{Im}U`$ at time $`2t`$, followed by a fixed passive transformation of two modes, gives amplitudes $`\sqrt t e^{i\theta}`$ and $`\sqrt t e^{-i\theta}`$ on an eigenphase $`\theta`$. Discarding the second mode and system yields the target complementary channel, including references. This is an explicit reduction derived here from the prior Gaussian purification, not a theorem stated in that work. It is detailed in [Gaussian comparison](../science/gaussian_connection.md). Implementing the Hermitian operators from $`U`$ ordinarily uses both $`U`$ and $`U^\dagger`$; the direct Poisson construction below establishes its own forward-only query bound.

There is also a straightforward forward-only phase-estimation comparison. Once the system is traced out, the output is a mixture of coherent states $`|\sqrt t\,e^{i\theta}\rangle`$, with spectral weights that may be correlated with the reference. Estimating $`\theta`$ to error $`O(\epsilon/\sqrt t)`$ with failure probability $`O(\epsilon)`$, then preparing the corresponding known coherent state, gives uniform complementary-output error $`O(\epsilon)`$. Standard phase estimation uses $`O((\sqrt t/\epsilon)\log(1/\epsilon))`$ forward queries for this accuracy and confidence; known output-preparation costs and error are separate. This is an elementary consequence derived here, detailed in the [comparison proof](../science/gaussian_connection.md), rather than an exact complementary-channel theorem attributed to the phase-estimation literature. Thus the fixed-error square-root scale, including forward access, is already explained by a familiar primitive.

The direct Poisson construction gives logarithmic precision dependence for this complementary-output task. With a fixed dilation and forward controlled $`U`$ access, a concentration interval $`[a,b]`$ can be implemented using only the powers $`U^{n-a}`$. The common factor $`U^a`$ acts on the system that is discarded. For $`0\lt \varepsilon\leq1/2`$, this gives

```math
O\!\left(\sqrt{t\log(1/\varepsilon)}+\log(1/\varepsilon)+1\right)
```

oracle calls, with the guarantee preserved in the presence of an arbitrary reference. At fixed error, the worst-case complementary-output cost is $`\Theta(\sqrt t)`$, while the corresponding worst-case reduced-system cost is $`\Theta(t)`$. The latter comparison holds in the same controlled-unitary model already for a qubit; [the elementary comparison proof](../science/interpretation.md#reduced-system-output-has-linear-query-complexity-in-the-same-model) establishes it directly. The stated optimality concerns the time dependence at fixed error; it does not assert matching precision dependence.

## Superreplication and typical phase windows

[Dür, Sekatski, and Skotiniotis][dur2015] explicitly center a typical Hamming-weight interval in their deterministic phase-gate superreplication protocol. Their Eqs. (5)–(6) reproduce phases within that interval after removing a common phase. This is the direct precedent for the scalar phase-centering mechanism used here. Their approximation is assessed through process fidelity, which corresponds to average input fidelity; their discussion of metrology also identifies inputs on which the approximation fails.

[Chiribella, Yang, and Huang][chiribella2015] extend gate superreplication to arbitrary unknown unitary gates using concentration in representation sectors. [Chiribella and Yang][chiribella2016] explain the relations among replication, estimation, and state generation, and distinguish average-input replication from its worst-input counterpart. The present result uses these familiar concentration and phase-clock ideas. Its target is a complementary channel on every input and reference, rather than an approximation to many parallel uses of a gate on typical inputs. The channel guarantee follows because the Poisson tail bound is independent of the system input and because a common unitary on the discarded system leaves the retained output unchanged.

## Quantum output and metrology

The information carried by an emitted field has an established theory. [Guţă and Kiukas][guta2017] analyze identifiable dynamical parameters and the local asymptotic normality of both joint system-output and reduced-output models for ergodic quantum Markov dynamics. [Macieszczak et al.][macieszczak2016] connect dynamical phase transitions with quantum Fisher information that grows quadratically in observation time; their enhanced regime explicitly involves joint system-output information. These works establish that the system and its output can have different information content, and that open-system metrology can exhibit enhanced temporal scaling.

Here, a finite-distance discrimination experiment converts output sensitivity into an oracle-query lower bound. For each requested horizon $`t`$, a known observer jump $`Z/\sqrt t`$ is added to the unknown qubit jump $`U_\theta=e^{-i\theta X}`$. The observer emits one photon on average over the full interval. Its reduced output alone distinguishes two oracles separated by $`O(1/t)`$, with constant bias, through a projector onto one photon in the uniform temporal mode. The final system and the other bath are discarded. The projector conserves photon number, so this lower bound survives dephasing between different total-photon-number sectors.

The resulting $`\Omega(t)`$ query requirement identifies a boundary of the complementary-output fast forwarding. It is an explicit existence result for a known noncommuting observer, not a claim that every noncommuting perturbation has this effect. Its rate decreases with $`t`$, while its integrated activity remains one. Moreover, the fully measured photon-count records of these unitary-jump models have oracle-independent Poisson laws. The lower bound therefore concerns coherence in the quantum output rather than the cost of sampling those classical records.

The condition $`H=0`$ fixes the stated microscopic coupling but does not remove coherent drift: $`\mathrm{Ad}_{e^{-i\theta X}}-\mathrm{id}=-i\theta[X,\cdot]+O(\theta^2)`$. At $`\theta=O(1/t)`$ the accumulated rotation remains finite. The observer transfers this sensitivity into temporal coherence of its emitted field. The result is an oracle-complexity boundary with a mean-one output and an explicit number-conserving test, not a new temporal metrology scaling law.

## Access and output conventions

The phase-referenced controlled oracle and the fixed output dilation are part of the problem definition. A reduced Lindblad generator alone does not specify an absolute field-phase convention. In the one-jump model, dephasing the output completely in photon number removes its dependence on $`U`$; this is a different target from the coherent complementary channel.

The upper bound prepares an encoded mode state. Oracle-query savings do not remove the energy or hardware cost of producing a physical field with mean photon number $`t`$. The observer lower bound keeps mean photon number equal to one, so that lower bound does not arise from a growing photon budget.
